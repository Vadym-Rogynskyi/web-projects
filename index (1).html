The case for one published racquets week

A one-page proposal: the problem, what this prototype does about it, how a pilot would run, and how we would know it worked. Live figures below come from the sample week on this page, not from the club.

Today

  • The three-sport scope is published in a job posting, not where members look.
  • Public directories disagree on how many tennis courts the club has.
  • Platform tennis is played at neighbouring clubs by arrangement, and nothing shows it in one place.
  • There is no rule that stops an out-of-date week from being shown as current.
→

With this calendar

  • One week, three sports, in a week grid and an agenda a member can read at a glance.
  • Every entry passes five checks before a member can see it.
  • Nothing publishes until the Director approves the week, in one click.
  • Every number on the page says where it came from and how sure it is.

Do the checks actually catch anything?

A check that always passes looks exactly like a check that does nothing. So the sample week has one failure planted for each check, on purpose, and this panel reads the live result of the build. If a check stopped working, its row would turn red here before anyone noticed on court.

Held for review

What the build caught before publication. These are not on the member calendar above — that is the whole point of the gate.

Approvals and overrides

Every override is recorded here with the reason given at the moment it was made, not reconstructed afterwards. A gate that can be opened without leaving a trace is not a control.

How the calendar is kept current

Five checks run every time the week is rebuilt. Each one exists because of a specific way a shared calendar goes wrong.

Check 1
Court conflict
No two sessions on one court whose times overlap, even by a few minutes. The classic failure of a schedule kept in several places.
Check 2
Court availability
Nothing scheduled on a court construction has closed that week. Through 2026 this changes constantly.
Check 3
Source freshness
Every entry carries the day its source was last confirmed. Past seven days it is held rather than republished on faith.
Check 4
Three-sport coverage
Every sport appears on each day it is promised to members, or the gap is named. A quiet sport shows as an absence, not as nothing.
Check 5
Approval
Nothing publishes without the director's yes, and an entry added after the yes waits for a new one. Building the week is nearly free, which is why the yes is the product.

Contradictions found in our own page, and fixed

The pitch is "trust this single view", so an inconsistency in our own sample data breaks it before a real club touches it. Build 14 went looking for them with a script that cross-checks the data against itself and against the page text.

  • Tennis courts. The facts ledger said "2 or 3" while the map drew 3. The page now uses 3 everywhere: map, text, ledger and checks. The dispute between directories is kept as a question for the Director, not as two numbers on the page.
  • Our own scope document. The midterm scope document said 2 tennis courts. It was one of the conflicting sources. It is superseded, and the notes now say so.
  • "Three sources, three answers." The sources give 3, 2 and 2. That is two answers, and the sentence now says so.
  • Confirmation dates. Tennis entries (High school prep (Tue), Mixed doubles social (Thu), Adult drill and play (Sun)) said they were confirmed 3 days ago, but no tennis source was 3 days old. They now match the pro shop clinic sheet, 2 days.
  • "Five layouts". Two places said five when the switcher had seven. Both now name the two member views and treat the rest as Director views or extras.
  • Check 1 compared text, not time. Two sessions on one court at 9:00–10:30 and 9:30–11:00 passed, because the strings differ. It now compares minutes and reports the overlap.
  • Check 4 could never fire. It only asked whether a sport appeared at all that week. It now also checks each day a sport is promised to members.
  • Check 5 was all or nothing. An entry added after the Director's approval would have published on the old yes. It is now held until a new approval.

What the public record shows that the club's site does not

Five findings from public sources, gathered November 18, 2026. Each one changes something in this build. Facts and source credit only — no club content, copy, photography or styling is reproduced here, and none of the sample session data below comes from the club.

1. The club publishes its racquets scope in hiring, not to members.

The club's own job posting for an Assistant Tennis Professional describes the role as supporting the Director of Racquets across lessons, clinics, pro shop, tournaments and exhibitions for a tennis, paddle and pickleball program, and routes applicants to a dedicated racquets address at the club. So the three-sport scope this calendar is built around is confirmed by the club itself — in a recruiting channel, not in the place a member would look. The address itself is not reproduced here, for the same reason this page takes no bookings.

That sharpens the thesis. The problem is not that the club lacks a racquets operation to publish, or lacks language for it. It has both, and routes them to candidates rather than to members.

Sources: Rumson Country Club job posting, Assistant Tennis Professional, via Indeed and Salary.com listings observed November 18, 2026.

2. Public court counts disagree with each other.

Global Tennis Network lists the club with three outdoor hard courts. Gladiator Tennis lists two. The midterm scope document for this project says two tennis courts alongside four pickleball courts and platform. Three sources, two answers, and one of them was this project's own document. Build 14 uses 3 everywhere and treats the scope document's 2 as superseded; only the club can settle it.

This is the strongest external evidence for check 3 in the engine. The freshness check was designed on the assumption that stale court data circulates and gets acted on. It does — in public directories a member or a visiting player could reasonably find first. A calendar that states its own source date and refuses to publish past the seven-day limit is answering a real failure, not a hypothetical one.

Sources: Global Tennis Network and Gladiator Tennis court directories, observed November 18, 2026. Neither is the club, and neither states when its figure was last confirmed — which is the point.

3. Platform play crosses club boundaries by arrangement.

Seabright Lawn Tennis & Cricket Club, roughly two miles away, publishes that it extends reciprocal platform tennis privileges to Rumson Country Club and Navesink Country Club. Reciprocity is a published, standing arrangement between neighbouring clubs, not an occasional favour.

That is a boundary this calendar does not model, and should say so rather than quietly assume otherwise. Every session in this engine is held on a court the club controls. A member's platform game may legitimately be on another club's deck, invisible to a court-conflict check that only knows about the club's own courts. Week 7 should test what the engine does with a session it cannot see, and the member view should not imply that what it shows is all the racquets play available that week.

Source: Seabright Lawn Tennis & Cricket Club public platform tennis page, observed November 18, 2026.

4. A neighbour publishes the season dates this one does not.

The same neighbouring club publishes a platform season of October 1 through March 31, with courts available year-round from 8am to 10pm. Those are real published dates from a club on the same peninsula, in the same climate, for the same sport.

The season boundaries in this prototype were invented. A local comparable does not make them correct, but it gives the Week 8 defense something better than an assumption: a published benchmark to state the sample data against, and a reason to ask the director for the club's actual dates rather than guess at them.

Source: Seabright Lawn Tennis & Cricket Club public platform tennis page, observed November 18, 2026. The season dates in this prototype's sample data remain invented and are not the club's.

5. The expansion claim is checkable in municipal records.

The scope document for this project cites four additional tennis courts and a new racquets shop planned for 2026 — sourced from a podcast, not from the club. But the club has taken facility work through the Rumson Borough Planning Board before: a 2021 proposal to expand the main clubhouse on its 210-acre site was heard by that board and covered in the local press.

So a racquets expansion of that size would generate public municipal records — agendas, applications, minutes. That converts the weakest citation in the scope document from unverifiable hearsay into something a reviewer could check, and gives a specific place to look before Week 8 rather than a claim to defend as-is.

Sources: Two River Times coverage of a Rumson Country Club clubhouse proposal before the Rumson Borough Planning Board, February 2021. The 2026 racquets expansion itself has not been verified in municipal records and is still a podcast-sourced claim.

The question that comes before any of this

How does the club schedule golf tee times today — and does that system already hold court bookings?

Private clubs this size generally already run a club-management platform, and court booking is a shipped module in those platforms rather than a gap in them. Jonas Club Software reports serving more than 2,300 clubs across 20+ countries and sells court and class booking alongside tee times, with booking rules by member status and slots by court type and day of week. Clubessential, Northstar and Club Caddie cover similar ground.

The club's public site carries a Clubessential credit, which makes this question sharper than it was. A website vendor is not proof of a booking module, but it narrows what to ask: does the club's Clubessential instance already hold court bookings alongside tee times? That is a question the golf shop can answer in one reply, and the answer decides whether Week 6 automates this calendar or reports that the club already owns the thing it would automate.

So this project does not build a booking engine. If the club already has one, rebuilding it would be spending a term re-creating something they already pay for. What those systems do not appear to produce is the thing above: one published view of the whole racquets week across three sports.

And if it turns out the incumbent platform does produce that view — then this project has no problem left to solve, and the right answer is to say so rather than build a second version of it.
