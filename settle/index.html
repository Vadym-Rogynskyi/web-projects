    How this is calculated

    For each city we start from a median 1-bedroom rent and a baseline home price (sized for a typical 3-bedroom), then scale both by bedroom count using standard rent and price premiums per extra bedroom. Bedrooms recommended follow a simple rule: about one bedroom per two people.

    The buy estimate assumes a 30-year fixed mortgage at your chosen rate and down payment, plus each state's typical effective property tax rate, ~0.35% annual insurance, ~1% annual maintenance, mortgage insurance (PMI) if you put down less than 20%, and an HOA estimate in condo- and co-op-heavy markets.

    The verdict weighs the price-to-rent ratio (under 15 tends to favor buying, over 25 tends to favor renting), your planned years in the home (buying rarely pays off in under 3 years once closing costs are counted), and whether either option would eat an uncomfortable share of your income.

    All of this is a simplified teaching model with estimated, non-live data — a real decision should start with current local listings and a conversation with a lender.
