What each session means

Easy
Conversational pace

You can talk in full sentences. Most of your running is here. It builds the engine without wearing you out.

Intervals
Short, fast repeats

Warm up 1.5 km easy, run the repeats, jog the recoveries, cool down 1.5 km. Makes race pace feel easier.

Tempo
Comfortably hard

You could say a few words but not chat. Teaches you to hold a strong pace for a long time.

Long run
Slow and steady

The most important run of the week. Builds endurance so the last 3 km of the race feel strong.

Strength
25–30 minutes

Strong legs and core keep your form together when you're tired and lower injury risk.

Rest
Part of the plan

Fitness is built while you recover. A walk or gentle stretching is fine.

Strides
4–6 × 20 seconds

After an easy run: smooth, quick, relaxed accelerations with a full walk back. Good for form and leg speed.

Race-day pacing

Most runners start a 10K too fast and pay for it later. Run the first 2 km a little slower than goal pace, settle in, and save your push for the last 3 km.

Race week playbook

Mon–Thu
  • Cut distance, keep a little speed (see week 7).
  • Sleep 8 hours if you can; it matters more than any workout now.
  • Drink water steadily through the day.
Fri · day before
  • Rest, or a 15-minute walk.
  • Lay out kit: shoes you've already run in, socks, bib, 4 safety pins, watch charged.
  • Normal dinner you know works for you. Nothing new.
  • Check start time, route to the start and where to park or drop bags.
Race morning
  • Eat 2–3 hours before: toast or oats with banana, a coffee if you usually have one.
  • Arrive 45–60 minutes early. Toilet queue first.
  • Warm up 10 minutes easy jog plus 3–4 strides, finishing 10 minutes before the start.
The race
  • Km 1–2: hold back, about 5 seconds per km slower than goal.
  • Km 3–7: settle on goal pace. Relax shoulders, short quick steps on hills.
  • Km 8–10: if you feel good, push. Last 400 m, go.
After
  • Keep walking 10 minutes; drink and eat something within an hour.
  • Sunday: walk or 20 minutes very easy. Then celebrate.

Golden rules

Easy means easy

About 80% of your running should be at conversational pace. Going too hard on easy days ruins the hard days.

Consistency wins

Missed a run? Skip it and carry on. Never squeeze two hard sessions together to catch up.

Nothing new on race day

Shoes, breakfast, gels and kit should all be tested on long runs first.

Pain is a stop sign. Normal tiredness and stiffness are fine. Sharp pain, pain that gets worse while you run, or pain that makes you limp means stop and take 2–3 days off. If it stays, see a doctor or physio before running again.
