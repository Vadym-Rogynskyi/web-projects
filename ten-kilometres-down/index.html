The Sunlight Zone

Almost all the ocean life you've ever seen lives here. Sunlight reaches deep enough for photosynthesis, and microscopic phytoplankton make roughly half the oxygen on Earth.

1,000 m
IIMesopelagic · 200–1,000 m

The Twilight Zone

Light fades to a dim blue, too weak for plants. Every night, billions of animals swim up from here to feed near the surface and sink back before dawn. It is the largest migration on the planet.

  • SwordfishWarm their eyes and brain with a special heater organ to hunt in the cold.

IIIBathypelagic · 1,000–4,000 m

The Midnight Zone

No sunlight reaches this far. The only light is made by animals: about three quarters of the creatures in the open water here can glow. The submersible's lamps switch on automatically.

IVAbyssopelagic · 4,000–6,000 m

The Abyss

Near-freezing, pitch black and crushing. Abyssal plains, flat stretches of soft sediment, cover about half of Earth's surface. Almost all of it has never been seen.

VHadopelagic · 6,000–10,935 m

The Hadal Zone

Named after Hades. These are the ocean trenches, deep scars where one tectonic plate dives beneath another. The walls close in, and the floor is the deepest place on Earth.

Challenger Deep · Mariana Trench

10,935 metres.
The bottom.

Put Mount Everest here and its summit would still sit more than two kilometres under water. The water above you presses down with over a thousand times the pressure at the surface. Only a handful of people have ever been here.
