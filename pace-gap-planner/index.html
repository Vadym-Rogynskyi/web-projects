Why these three levers

  • A long run pace is not your race pace. Long runs are meant to be easy, usually 45–110 s/mile slower than race pace. Treating 10:45 as your current race pace overstates the gap; the effort toggle shows the difference.
  • Weeks set how many training blocks you get. Gains shrink over time, so doubling the weeks doesn't double the improvement.
  • Runs per week matter more than the single speed session: total weekly running is the main driver of endurance gains.
  • Goal time is the lever you control on race day. A small change here can turn "unlikely" into "on track".
  • Race distance isn't a lever: it's fixed once you've entered.
